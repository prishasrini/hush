Your privacy in Hush

Use a nickname and avoid sharing identifying details. Hush is a college project. It does not provide end-to-end encryption or guarantee complete anonymity.

What is saved

We store your username, a salted password hash, and an account ID. Your actual password is not saved. Mood check-ins and notes, peer-room posts, and counsellor requests are saved in Supabase PostgreSQL and linked to your account ID.

Who can read saved content

Hush administrators can access stored entries. Our hosting and database providers operate the infrastructure; we cannot promise that saved content is unreadable to them. Other users cannot access your private mood history through Hush, but room participants can read and copy peer-room posts.

Talking to Juno

Hush does not save new Juno conversations in its database. Your current conversation stays in this page's memory and is sent through Hush to OpenRouter and its AI providers to generate replies. Refreshing or leaving the page clears it from the chat interface. This does not guarantee deletion from provider systems, and older chats previously saved by Hush are not automatically removed.

AI providers have their own data retention and processing policies. Read OpenRouter's privacy policy. Do not share information you are uncomfortable sending to an external AI service.

Voice input

Voice input uses your browser's speech recognition service, which may send audio to its provider. Typed chat is available if you prefer not to use voice.

Deleting your mood history

On your dashboard, choose “Delete my mood history” and confirm. This permanently removes your mood check-ins and notes from Hush's active database, while keeping your account. Provider backups may retain older copies until they expire; this button does not erase backups, room posts, counsellor requests, or old chat records.

Login sessions

Hush uses a signed login cookie to keep you signed in. Sign out when using a shared device.
